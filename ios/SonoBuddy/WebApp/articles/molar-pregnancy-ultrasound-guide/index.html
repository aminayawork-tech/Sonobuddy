Molar Pregnancy on Ultrasound: Complete vs. Partial Mole

How gestational trophoblastic disease looks on ultrasound — the snowstorm uterus, theca lutein cysts, how a partial mole differs from a complete mole, and why early moles are so easy to miss.

OBfirst trimestermolar pregnancyGTDpathology

Molar pregnancy, or hydatidiform mole, is the most common form of gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD). It's an abnormal growth of placental tissue, and it can turn up on what was booked as a routine dating scan or a "rule out miscarriage" exam. Seeing it is what sends the patient for serial hCG follow-up after evacuation, because a small share of moles go on to become persistent or invasive disease.

Complete vs. Partial Mole

The two types differ in genetics, in appearance, and in what you'll see on the screen.

FeatureComplete molePartial mole
GeneticsDiploid, all paternal DNAUsually triploid (69 chromosomes)
