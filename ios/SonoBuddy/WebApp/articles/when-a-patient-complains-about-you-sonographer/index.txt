3:Tafa,The email from your manager says, "Can we chat about a patient concern?" Your stomach drops.

If you scan long enough, you **will** get a complaint. About pressure on a sore belly, waiting time, not "telling them what you saw," a cold room, or the gel. Some are fair. Many aren't. Here's how to handle it without losing sleep, or your confidence.

![When a patient complains about you](/blog/career/patient-complaint.svg "Five steps for handling a patient complaint|SonoBuddy")

## Why Sonographers Get Complaints

- **We can't share results.** Patients read silence as hiding something.
- **Exams can hurt.** Pressure on a tender gallbladder, a full bladder, transvaginal scans.
- **Expectations are high**, especially at OB scans, around 3D pictures, the baby's sex, or "cute" photos.
- **Delays** from add-ons and ER emergencies land on whoever's in the room.
- **People are scared.** Fear often comes out as anger at the nearest person.

None of that makes a complaint pleasant, but it explains why good sonographers get them too.

## The First 24 Hours

1. **Don't respond in the moment.** Breathe. Don't message the patient or vent online.
2. **Write down what you remember**, while it's fresh: times, what was said, who else was there.
3. **Talk to your lead or manager the same day.** Hearing your side early helps.
4. **Check your documentation.** Chaperone noted? Pain or limitations noted? Good charting protects you.

## Separate Fact From Feeling

Ask yourself honestly:

- Is there **something true** in it? (*"She didn't explain what she was doing."*)
- Is it about **something outside my control**? (Wait times, results policy, insurance.)
- Is it a **pattern** or a one-off?

If there's a fair point, own it. Small fixes like explaining each step, offering a warm towel, or checking in about pressure go a long way.

## Prevent the Next One

- **Narrate the exam:** *"I'm going to press a little here; tell me if it's too much."*
- **Set expectations up front** on results, timing, and what the scan can and can't show.
- **Use a chaperone** for sensitive exams, and document it.
- **Know your script** for "what do you see?" (see our guide on [what to say when the patient asks](/blog/patient-asks-what-do-you-see)).

## Protect Your Confidence

- **One complaint doesn't define you.** Experienced sonographers have dozens of grateful patients for every complaint.
- **Talk to coworkers.** Almost all of them have a story.
- **Watch for burnout signs.** If complaints start feeling crushing, it may be exhaustion talking (see [Sonographer Stress and Burnout](/blog/sonographer-stress-and-burnout)).

## The Bottom Line
