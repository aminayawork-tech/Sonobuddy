Yes, plenty of sonographers earn six figures. But it doesn't happen by default. The spread between the lowest- and highest-paid sonographers is huge, and a few choices explain most of it.

![Sonographer pay range](/blog/career/sonographer-six-figures.svg "Sonographer pay range|SonoBuddy. Wages: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, May 2024")

## The Numbers

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (May 2024), for diagnostic medical sonographers:

| | Annual pay |
|---|---|
| **Bottom 10%** | Under about **$64,760** |
| **Median** | **$89,340** |
| **Top 10%** | Over about **$123,170** |

So six figures puts you somewhere in the top third or so of the field. That's very achievable, but you have to aim for it.

## Who Earns the Least

Lower pay usually comes from a combination of:

- **New graduates** in their first year or two
- **Low cost-of-living, rural areas**
- **Small physician offices and outpatient clinics** with no shift differentials
- **A single credential** and no specialty
- **Day shift only**, with no call or overtime

None of these is permanent. They're mostly about where you are in your career and where you work.

## Five Paths to Six Figures

### 1. Specialize
**Cardiac (echo) and vascular** sonographers often earn more than general sonographers, and **pediatric echo, fetal echo, and MSK** are niche skills that command a premium. See [Highest Paid Sonography Specialties](/blog/highest-paid-sonography-specialties).

### 2. Stack credentials
Each extra credential (for example, RDMS plus RVT, or RDCS plus RVT) makes you more valuable, more flexible to schedule, and harder to replace. Multi-credentialed sonographers can cover more exam types and often negotiate higher pay.

### 3. Work where pay is high
West Coast states such as **California** typically top state pay rankings, and large metro hospitals generally pay more than rural clinics. Weigh this against cost of living.

### 4. Take differentials, call, and overtime
Evening, night, weekend, and **on-call** pay add up fast. Hospital sonographers who take call regularly often clear six figures in markets where the base rate alone wouldn't.

### 5. Travel
**Travel contracts** pay a premium (often including stipends) to fill short-term gaps. Two or three years of travel can raise your earnings and your resume. See [Sonographer Travel Assignment Pay](/blog/sonographer-travel-assignment-pay).

Longer term, **lead sonographer, management, education, and applications/sales roles** with equipment vendors can also pass six figures.

## Which Sonography Specialty Is in Most Demand?

Demand is strong across the field: BLS projects sonographer employment to grow **13% from 2024 to 2034**, much faster than average. In job postings, **general (abdomen and OB/GYN), cardiac, and vascular** sonographers are consistently the most sought after, and multi-credentialed candidates have the most options. More in our [2026 job market outlook](/blog/sonographer-job-market-2026-outlook).

## The Bottom Line

The median sonographer earns about **$89,000**. To reach **$100,000+**, combine two or three of these: **a specialty, extra credentials, a high-paying market, differentials or call, and travel.** Most sonographers who get there do it within five to ten years.

*Figures are BLS national estimates for May 2024. Local pay varies widely.*
