"Should I do nursing or sonography?" is one of the most common questions from people choosing a healthcare career. Both are good jobs with solid pay. They're also very different jobs, and "harder" depends a lot on what you're good at.

![Sonography vs nursing comparison](/blog/career/sonography-vs-nursing.svg "Sonography vs nursing at a glance|SonoBuddy. Median pay: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, May 2024")

## The Short Answer

- **Pay:** close. The BLS median was **$93,600 for registered nurses** and **$89,340 for diagnostic medical sonographers** (May 2024). Your specialty, location, and shifts matter more than the title.
- **School:** nursing covers **more material**; sonography goes **deeper in fewer subjects** and is often **harder to get into**.
- **Daily work:** nursing is broad patient care; sonography is focused imaging, one patient at a time.

## Getting In

**Nursing** programs are everywhere: community colleges (ADN), universities (BSN), and accelerated programs for career changers. Competitive programs exist, but there are many seats nationwide.

**Sonography** programs are fewer and usually admit **small classes**, because each student needs scanning-lab time and a clinical placement. It's common to apply to several programs, or more than once. Look for **CAAHEP-accredited** programs, which give you the most direct route to registry exams.

## What's Hard About Each

### Nursing school
- A huge volume of content: pharmacology, pathophysiology, health assessment, and clinical judgment across every body system
- Care plans, dosage calculations, and constant exams
- The **NCLEX-RN** licensing exam at the end
- Clinicals that can involve heavy patient care, emotional situations, and shift work

### Sonography school
- **Ultrasound physics**, which most students say is the hardest class (see our [guide to sonography school](/blog/is-sonography-school-hard))
- **Cross-sectional anatomy** in every plane, and recognizing it in grayscale
- **Hand-eye scanning skill**, which only comes with hours of practice
- Long, often **unpaid clinical rotations**, frequently close to full time
- The **ARDMS SPI** physics exam plus at least one specialty exam (abdomen, OB/GYN, vascular, or cardiac) to become registered

If you're strong at spatial reasoning and like mastering one skill deeply, sonography may feel easier. If you prefer variety and people-centered care over technical work, nursing may.

## Pay and Earning Potential

Both medians sit in the high $80Ks to low $90Ks. In both careers, the biggest pay jumps come from:

- **Specialty** (for example, cardiac or vascular sonography; ICU or OR nursing)
- **Location**, with West Coast states typically paying the most
- **Shift differentials, call, and overtime**
- **Travel contracts**

Nurses have a higher ceiling through advanced degrees (nurse practitioner, CRNA). Sonographers can grow pay with extra credentials, travel, leadership, or education roles. See [Can You Make Six Figures as a Sonographer?](/blog/sonographer-six-figure-salary)

## Flexibility

Nursing wins on **flexibility**. RNs work in hospitals, clinics, schools, home health, telehealth, insurance, and dozens of specialties, and switching specialties is relatively easy.

Sonographers mostly work in **imaging**: hospitals, outpatient centers, physician offices, and mobile services. Moving between specialties usually means a new credential.

## The Physical and Emotional Side

- **Nursing:** on your feet for 12-hour shifts, patient lifting and transfers, high patient loads, and frequent end-of-life care.
- **Sonography:** repetitive strain from scanning. The **shoulder, wrist, neck, and back** take the hit (see our [ergonomics series](/blog/sonographer-wrist-pain-scanning)). Emotionally hard moments also come up, like finding a fetal demise or a new cancer, often while the patient is watching your face.

## Which Should You Choose?

**Choose sonography if** you love anatomy and visual puzzles, want deep expertise in one skill, and prefer one-on-one patient time.

**Choose nursing if** you want the widest range of jobs, enjoy broad hands-on patient care, and may want to go on to an advanced degree.

Either way, **shadow both** before you apply. One day in each is the best tiebreaker there is.

*Salary figures are BLS national medians for May 2024; local pay varies widely.*
