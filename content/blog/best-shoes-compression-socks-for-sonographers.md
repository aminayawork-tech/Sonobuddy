"Best shoes for sonographers" is one of the most-searched questions about this job, and for good reason. You stand, shift, lean and reach for 8 to 12 hours. Your feet are the foundation your scanning arm stands on.

This isn't a paid list. It's what to look for, so you can choose what fits **your** feet.

![Shoes and gear for sonographers](/blog/career/sonographer-shoes.svg "Five things that matter in sonographer footwear|SonoBuddy")

## What to Look For in a Scanning Shoe

1. **Cushioning and arch support.** The most important feature. Long periods of standing on hard hospital floors punish thin soles.
2. **Slip-resistant soles.** Gel, fluids and freshly mopped floors are everywhere.
3. **A wipeable, fluid-resistant upper.** Mesh looks nice until something spills on it.
4. **A roomy toe box.** Feet swell over a long shift.
5. **A stable heel.** Avoid anything that rolls your ankle when you pivot to the monitor.

**Popular choices** among healthcare workers include cushioned running and walking shoes from brands like Hoka, Brooks and New Balance, plus professional clogs like Dansko. Popular doesn't mean right for you, though: fit always wins.

## Make Them Last

- **Rotate two pairs** so the cushioning recovers between shifts.
- **Replace them when the cushioning feels flat**, often within a year for daily work shoes, and sooner if your knees or back start aching.
- **Add quality insoles** if you need more arch support. See a podiatrist for custom orthotics if you have persistent foot pain.

## Compression Socks

Many sonographers swear by compression socks for long standing shifts. They gently squeeze the legs to help blood return toward the heart, which can reduce swelling and leg fatigue.

- **Mild compression (often 15–20 mmHg)** is commonly used for everyday wear on long shifts.
- **Check with your doctor first** if you have circulation problems, diabetes, or any vascular condition, or before using higher compression.

## Small Upgrades That Add Up

- **Anti-fatigue mats** where you stand to scan
- **Sit-stand variety:** alternate positions when the exam allows
- **A stool with lumbar support** for long exams
- **Stretch your calves and feet** between patients

## Your Feet Affect Everything Else

Bad footwear changes how you stand, which changes how you reach, which loads your **back and shoulder**. Pair good shoes with good scanning ergonomics: see our guides on [lower back](/blog/sonographer-lower-back-pain-scanning), [shoulder](/blog/sonographer-shoulder-pain-30-degree-rule), and [wrist](/blog/sonographer-wrist-pain-scanning) pain.

## The Bottom Line

Choose **cushioned, supportive, slip-resistant, wipeable** shoes that fit your feet. **Rotate and replace them**, consider **mild compression socks**, and treat your feet like the equipment they are.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. See a podiatrist or physician for persistent foot, leg, or back pain.*
